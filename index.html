Honest limits

  • Permits are not all the work. Unpermitted jobs, work outside city limits (suburbs, county land) and work that doesn't need a permit won't show up.
  • Coverage differs by city. Austin: building, electrical, mechanical, plumbing and driveway permits. Chicago: all building permits, with every listed contractor. NYC: DOB NOW Build initial work permits plus DOB NOW Electrical. Seattle: building, electrical and trade permits. Seattle's building permits rarely name a contractor, so about a quarter of Seattle permits are unattributed.
  • Names are free text. We merge case, punctuation, LLC/Inc/Co suffixes, d/b/a names and names the city's system cut off, but one company can still appear under two spellings.
  • Trades are inferred. Cities don't tag a permit "roofing" or "solar". We assign one trade per permit from the permit type, contractor role and description. Big trades are reliable; small ones are directional.
  • Job values are what was declared. Many permits carry $0 or $1 placeholders, and trade sub-permits usually carry none. We show values only where the city recorded one. They are not revenue.
  • "New" means new to the 12-month record. A contractor flagged new had no permit in the city in the 11 months before its first one this month. That doesn't always mean a new business.
  • Data lags a few days. Cities publish permits 1–3 business days after issue, so the most recent days are always a little light.
  • No personal data. We never show homeowner or property-owner names, owner contact details or street addresses. Only business and licensed-contractor names, ZIP codes and the city's own job descriptions (with phone numbers, emails and addresses removed).
  • Not a licence check or a recommendation. Verify licences and insurance with your state before you hire anyone.

FAQ

Where does the data come from?

Only from each city's official open-data portal: Austin (data.austintexas.gov), Chicago (data.cityofchicago.org), New York City (data.cityofnewyork.us, Department of Buildings) and Seattle (data.seattle.gov). We pull issued permits for the last 12 months and refresh every Monday. The exact datasets are listed under each city in the radar.

How fresh is it?

Each city's page shows its own "data as of" date: the latest issue date in that city's feed when we last built. Cities usually publish within 1–3 business days.

How is momentum worked out?

For each contractor we compare permits issued in the last 90 days with the 90 days before that. "Surging" means at least 50% more permits and at least 5 in the last 90 days; "slowing" means at least 15% fewer.

Why can't I find a contractor?

Try a shorter part of the name. Some permits are pulled by the general contractor rather than the trade sub, some by property owners themselves (we never show those), and work outside city limits isn't in the city's data.

Is this a background or licence check?

No. It shows permit activity recorded by the city, nothing more. A busy permit history isn't an endorsement, and an empty one isn't a red flag on its own: check licences, insurance and references too.

Why these four cities? What about Dallas or San Francisco?

We only launch a city when its official feed names the contractor on most permits. Dallas's feed stopped carrying contractor names in 2026. San Francisco moved most new permits to a new system in 2026 whose public data has no contractor field. We re-check both regularly and will add them when the data supports it.

What do the alert emails look like?

Every Monday: a short summary of permits in your trades and ZIPs, new contractors, the biggest movers up and down, and a CSV of every matching permit for the week. Plain text and HTML.

Can I cancel?

Yes. Plans are month to month. Reply to any alert email to cancel before your next billing date.
